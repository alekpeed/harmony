Build report · 16 August 2026

Harmony Gates

A jazz-harmony game for Android tablets where a real MIDI keyboard is the controller. All sixteen phases are built. There is a campaign of seventeen regions and forty-three gates — from a first note on a keyboard through to comping a chart — progress that survives closing the app and exports to a file, help that arrives one rung at a time, an ear-training engine with a synthesised piano behind it, a staff that grades pitch and rhythm separately, and a release path that produces a signed APK. What none of it has ever had is a keyboard.

New in phases 5 and 6

It remembers, and it decides what you have earned

A gate does not open because a counter reached twelve. It opens because the stored evidence satisfies a rule an author wrote — and that rule is re-checked from the evidence every time the map is drawn, so the two can never drift apart.

What one attempt is worth, from 06_PERFORMANCE_EVALUATION_AND_SCORING.md §9.
Evidence Weight What it means
INDEPENDENT_CORRECT1.0Right, with nothing on screen but the chord symbol
CORRECT_WITH_REDUCED_ASSISTANCE0.8Right, with some assistance still showing
CORRECT_AFTER_HINT0.5Right, after asking
INCORRECT0.0Wrong
Rule

The grade is stored, the weight is not

An attempt row records that you were helped, never what being helped is worth. Whether you had a hint is a fact and will not change; what a hint costs is a teaching judgement that might. Keeping them apart is what lets the whole history be re-scored under a new weighting instead of migrated by guesswork.

Rule

Nothing stores “complete”

The database records when a gate was first passed and never that it was. A stored flag can disagree with the attempts behind it; a derived one cannot. It is also what makes the map deterministic — same evidence, same campaign, every time.

Rule

It will not make you sit through the tutorial twice

A gate session is shaped around what you already know: guided trials for a first visit, challenge trials and a scored check for someone who has proved the skill, and the tutorial back again only for the player who is actually stuck.

Refused

A campaign that cannot be played

The validator rejects prerequisite cycles, gates nothing can ever unlock, references to content that does not exist, and a curriculum with no way in. It runs in CI against the curriculum that actually ships, not a fixture.

New in phase 10

A progression, coming towards you

Eight chords are visible; the run behind them can be any length. Play the one at the play point and every orb slides one place nearer, the far end recycling to carry the next chord in. Press the button to take one step — the path, the orb sizes and the 650 ms easing below are read from interface/maps/progression-run.json, the same file the app reads at runtime.

A correct chord advances the track. A wrong one does not.
Rule

The orb is a label, not the question

The run evaluates the chord event at activeChordIndex, never the symbol drawn inside the orb. In a rootless run those two differ on purpose: the orb says G7 and the answer is B–E–F–A, judged against a chord carrying the thirteenth and ninth the shape actually sounds.

Rule

One chord, one advance

Holding a voicing to hear it, or leaving the pedal down, cannot walk you through three chords you never played. After a chord is accepted the run stops listening until the keyboard is quiet — and does not wait at all if your hands are already off, which is how most chords finish.

Region 7

Several right answers

Shells, guide tones and the two rootless rotations are policies rather than fixed voicings, so any register and spacing is accepted. Rootless A and B contain identical notes, so playing the wrong one is reported as a wrong bass — which is what it is.

Not baked

The room stays still

Track, orbs, pedestals and labels are drawn over the background, never into it. No approved plate has been supplied yet, so today the track runs over the theme background and the export drops in later with no code change.

New in phase 7

Help arrives one rung at a time

The specification is blunt about this: a hint must never simply play the answer. So assistance is a ladder of eight rungs rather than a switch, and each rung names exactly what is on screen — the symbol, the note names, the keyboard targets, the staff. What a player earns is measured against the rung they were standing on.

Rule

A hint narrows; it does not answer

The ladder goes from “one of your notes is wrong” to “the seventh is wrong” to “the seventh should be a semitone lower”. The last rung still leaves the player something to do. A hint that reveals the chord is a different exercise, and one nobody learns from.

Consequence

Asking costs, and the cost is recorded

A correct answer after a hint is worth 0.5, against 1.0 independently. The mastery model already stored the grade rather than the weight, so the cost of asking arrived without touching a single stored row.

Recovery

Failing three times changes the exercise, not the player

Repeated failure drops the assistance level, offers the remedial policy the gate author already mapped, and re-asks. Nothing is marked as a defeat: the loop’s job is to get the player back to a rung they can stand on.

Not a difficulty menu

One dial, not four presets

Presets exist as named points on the same ladder rather than as separate modes, because a screen built per combination is exactly what the product scope forbids.

New in phase 8

It makes a sound now

A pure-Kotlin mixer feeding AudioTrack: voice pool, linear-interpolation resampling, envelope, per-voice gain, summed float buffer, soft limiter. No allocation in the render loop — the audio spec asks for that by name, and a garbage collection pause inside a callback is audible as a click.

Ear training

Four kinds of question, one evaluator

Interval, chord quality, progression and voicing-colour exercises all resolve to the same requirement type the chord gates use, so there is still exactly one place in the codebase that decides whether an answer is right.

Voice stealing

The oldest note goes, not the newest

A full voice pool drops the note that has been sounding longest rather than refusing the one just played. Refusing the new note makes the keyboard feel broken; dropping the old one is what a real instrument does.

New in phase 9

A staff, and two scores that move independently

Durations are exact rationals rather than floating-point seconds: three triplet eighths are one quarter here, and 0.9999999999999999 in doubles. Staff position comes from the letter and the octave, not from the MIDI number, so F and F♯ share a line and G♯ and A♭ do not.

Caught by a failing test

Pitch and rhythm had been welded together

The evaluator would not count a note as in time unless it was also the right note, which made both scores rise and fall as one and destroyed the distinction the whole engine exists for. A reader hitting wrong notes exactly on the beat has good time and a pitch problem, and now hears that.

Determinism

Grading needs no clock

Timestamps are handed in rather than read, so a perfect performance grades perfectly, instantly, and identically on every machine. Notes are claimed by nearest written time, so one missed note does not derail the rest of the bar.

New in phase 11

The harmony a player meets after the seventh chords

Regions 9 to 12 of the curriculum: quartal voicings, altered dominants, diminished systems and substitutions. All of it is written as transformation rather than as a table — C7♭9 is one operation applied to C7, not one of thirty entries that could disagree with each other.

Region 11

Three chords, not twelve

A fully diminished seventh divides the octave into four equal parts, so there are only three of them in all twelve keys and each can be spelled from any of its four notes. The engine can now say so, and prove it: every diminished seventh on every root is filed against the family it belongs to.

Region 12

A substitution only exists in context

D♭7 on its own is a chord. Dm7 D♭7 Cmaj7 is a tritone substitution. So the engine rewrites progressions rather than returning chords, and each substitution carries the tones the two chords share — the reason the ear accepts the swap.

Caught by a test

A substitute nobody could read

Transposing by a diminished fifth gave E♭7 a substitute of B♭♭7 — arithmetically correct, and a chord no chart has ever printed. It then had no substitute of its own, because its fifth needed a triple accidental. Roots now fall back to the chart spelling past one accidental: A7.

Caught by a test

The common-tone chord was on the wrong side

It was being built a semitone below the root, which is the leading-tone chord and shares nothing with a major triad. A semitone above — C – C♯dim7 – C — shares the third and the fifth, which is what the name means.

New in phase 12

The palette was sampled, not chosen

The colours come out of the two approved plates: warm near-blacks around #0A0908, brass and amber from #B07020 up to #F0C090, warm off-white text. A Compose layer drawn over painted artwork has to be in the artwork’s palette or it sits beside it rather than on it.

Decision

One palette, no light theme

A Compose layer that turned pale in light mode would be drawn on a plate that did not turn with it. Contrast is met inside the dark palette instead, and every text-on-surface pair is measured against WCAG AA by a test rather than eyeballed.

Structural, not aspirational

Key state maps to shape before colour

The nine layered piano-key states map to a fill, a marker and an outline through a function that has no access to the palette. So “do not encode these states only by color” is something a test can check: no two of the nine share both a marker and an outline.

Honest scope

Half of this phase is not code

The final Figma screens, the node-ID mapping and the screenshot baselines are design work. They are not done, they are not started, and nothing here invented them. Two screens are approved as painted plates; the rest have not been drawn.

Provisional

Two groups the artwork does not contain

Neither approved screen shows a verdict or a keyboard, so the feedback colours and the piano key surfaces are marked provisional in the token file. Nothing depends on their exact value, because every state also carries a glyph or an outline.

New in phase 13

A campaign you can walk end to end

Seventeen regions and forty-three gates, in curriculum order: a first note on a keyboard, then triads, sevenths, functional harmony, ear training, reading, jazz voicings, drops, extensions, sus and slash chords, quartal structures, altered dominants, diminished systems, substitutions, the progression vocabulary, voice leading, and comping a chart.

The point of the phase

A tune is a file, not a commit

Region 12's mandatory vocabulary — the blues, rhythm changes, descending dominants, the turnaround variants — is eleven entries in a JSON file, because a new parser reads roman numerals the way a chart writes them: ii7, b II7, #iv°7, V7/ii. Adding a standard is no longer a code change.

Engines that had no content

Ear training and reading are reachable

A policy can now name an ear-training family, a progression, or sight-reading material, and what kind of gate it is follows from which of those it names. The phase 8 and 9 engines had been finished and unreachable for two phases.

Caught by the content validator

A drop voicing is not a set of notes

Authoring the drop gates failed outright: a drop 2 is the same four tones with one moved down an octave, so there is no recipe to look up. It is named on the voicing policy instead, and judged on its spacing — close position is a different answer.

Acceptance

Every gate is on the path

A test walks the prerequisite graph from the calibration gate and asserts all forty-three gates are reachable and that the walk arrives at integrated reading. A campaign can be valid and still stop three regions short of where it was meant to.

New in phase 14

The quality pass that does not need a tablet

Seven deliverables. Three are done; four — tablet profiling, latency tuning, MIDI compatibility, audio tuning — need hardware, and are listed as blocked rather than quietly skipped.

Export and import

A backup carries evidence, not conclusions

Sessions, attempts and gate completions go into the file. Mastery does not — it is derived from the attempts, and a stored estimate could be restored into a build that weighted it differently and would disagree with its own history forever. The import rebuilds it with the same code that scores a played session.

Process death

Four numbers, not a session

The generator is deterministic, so a killed session needs its seed and its position, not its exercises. A half-written saved state starts fresh rather than resuming under an id that means something else, and the interrupted exercise is given again rather than marked wrong.

Measure rather than assume

Two budgets that run anywhere

Chord evaluation under 10 ms and exercise generation under 50 ms are the spec's own numbers, checked on every run — median of several timings after a warm-up, because a mean on a shared machine fails builds that are not broken. The other three budgets are latency, audio and frames, and they need the tablet.

Accessibility

A hint says what it costs

The progress bar announces its position, the task reads as one sentence rather than four fragments, and the hint button tells a player it counts against the exercise before they press it. One screen, honestly; the others have not been audited.

New in phase 15

There is a build you could install

assembleRelease produces a minified, resource-shrunk 3.9 MB APK, and a v* tag produces a signed APK and an App Bundle as CI artifacts. Nothing has installed any of them.

Signing

All four values, or none

Signing material comes from a gitignored properties file or from environment variables, never from the repository. With none present the release build still runs and is labelled unsigned — which still proves the shrinker and the resource stripper are happy on a machine with no secrets.

Three version numbers

The content version is read, not restated

App version, content version and content schema move independently. The latter two are read out of the authored curriculum at build time, so the version stamped on a stored attempt is the version of the content that produced it. A Kotlin constant would have gone stale the first time content changed.

Phase 1 · the substance

The engine spells chords the way a musician writes them

Incorrect theory silently ruins a training product, so this is where the testing went. The hard part is not which notes sound — it is which notes are written. These are real outputs; the accented note is the one that pitch-class arithmetic gets wrong.

Symbol Spelled The trap
Db7 Db  F  Ab  Cb Not B. The seventh of Db must sit on a C.
F#maj7 F#  A#  C#  E# Not F. Two Fs in one chord would be unreadable.
C7#9 C  E  G  Bb  D# Not Eb. A sharp ninth is a ninth, not a flat third.
Db7b9 Db  F  Ab  Cb  Ebb A double flat, and the correct answer.
Ebdim7 Eb  Gb  Bbb  Dbb Two double flats. Diminished sevenths are built in thirds.
C#m(maj7) C#  E  G#  B# Not C. The leading tone belongs on a B.
F#7#9 F#  A#  C#  E  Gx A double sharp, arrived at by the same one rule.

None of this is a lookup table. The degree number fixes the letter, and the arithmetic fixes the accidental — so all seven rows, and the other 353 chord/key pairs, come out of a single rule. All 74 hand-derived fixtures passed on the first run.

Also in the engine

Beyond spelling

  • 30 chord formulas from triads to altered dominants, each across twelve roots
  • Symbol parsing for Cm7b5, CΔ7, C7alt, Dbmaj9/F and their typed variants
  • Voicings generated against a policy — including rootless families, where "no root" is correct
  • Drop 2, drop 3, spread and inversion, as transformations rather than lookups
  • Voice leading scored by optimal assignment, so an added voice reads as added
  • Roman numerals: ii7 in C, V7/ii in E♭, the bII7 tritone sub, the backdoor cadence
Why it is separate

One place decides correctness

core:music is a plain Kotlin library with no Android dependency at all — a build-level guarantee, not a convention. The design system deliberately cannot see it either, so no piece of UI ever gets the chance to decide whether a chord is right.

It also runs as fast JVM tests, which is why several thousand theory assertions cost seconds rather than an emulator boot.

Phases 2 and 3 · the controller

Reading a keyboard, and deciding when a chord has been played

Two problems sit between a key going down and a verdict. The wire format is messier than it looks, and humans do not press a chord on one millisecond.

The parser

What real hardware actually sends

  • Running status — a fast chord omits the status byte after the first message, so 90 3C 64 3E 64 40 64 is three notes, not one and a mess
  • Real-time bytes mid-message — a clock byte is legal between the data bytes of another message; read as data it turns a C into something else
  • Split reads — the transport hands over whatever has arrived, which may be half a message
  • Note-on at velocity zero — how nearly every keyboard releases a key. Read as a note-on, every note sticks down forever.
The aggregator

Never submit on the first note

An evaluator handed the first note of a C major seventh would confidently mark it a C. So notes accumulate, and the chord is taken when the keyboard goes quiet, when every key comes up, when the roll window expires, or when something asks.

Thresholds are exercise configuration, not constants: the spec calls its 80 ms quiet window and 300 ms roll “starting default values for user testing”.

Three ways to be right

One comparison routine would have to pick one of these and be wrong about the other two.

The question Compared as What a naive check gets wrong
“Play any Cmaj7” Pitch classes Fails a correct answer played an octave lower
“Play this voicing” A multiset of notes A set agrees that a doubled root is the same as a single one
“Play a rootless G13” Chord degrees “Root missing means wrong” fails the whole of Region 7
Ranked, not listed

The most useful thing first

A player on the wrong chord who also spread it too wide is told about the chord. Mentioning the spread first would be technically complete and pedagogically useless. A missing guide tone outranks a missing fifth for the same reason: one changes what the chord is, the other usually does not.

Not a wrong answer

Two verdicts that cost nothing

A keyboard unplugged mid-chord returns ABORTED_DEVICE_LOSS, and silence returns NO_ATTEMPT. Neither is scored against the player, and neither teaches the mastery model anything it should not learn.

Phase 4 · the loop closes

A chord appears. You play it. It tells you what was wrong.

Deliberately plain. The phase asks for “one intentionally plain exercise screen”, and this is that — it exists to prove the stack holds together under fingers, not to look like the finished game.

One engine, not three

The session lifecycle exists once

Ear training and sight reading will attach to the same engine rather than growing their own copies of present-arm-capture-evaluate. It is plain Kotlin with no Android dependency, which is why the whole loop is unit-tested end to end against a fake keyboard.

That required moving the capture contract into the music module and leaving the MIDI implementation behind. A quiet window is a musical decision; a byte stream is not.

Content, not code

A lesson is a policy, not a screen

Root pool, chord qualities, inversions, register, how the answer is judged, what the player is shown — all data on an ExercisePolicy. Adding a lesson should not mean adding a screen, and after this phase it does not.

Two things worth knowing

What the tests caught

Found by a property test

A chord that cannot be written

A C♭ diminished seventh needs a B triple-flat, which standard notation has no way to write. The engine now reports that rather than handing back an A — the same sound, a different chord on the page, and a substitution no test would ever have caught.

Content must use B♭im7 instead. The content validator has enforced it since phase 6.

Found by inspection, then automated

An asset that was not an image

The first upload of the home artwork arrived as 14,997 bytes containing no JPEG markers at all. The build tool copies files without decoding them, so it would have shipped and surfaced as a blank screen on a tablet — a long way from its cause.

The build now reads image headers directly and fails with the reason. The valid export is in and verified.

Found by a scripted scenario

Letting go is how you finish

The filter that ignores an accidentally brushed key discarded every note of a short chord, because all of them are released quickly. A correct C major seventh evaluated as an empty attempt.

The rule now asks whether the key came up while the chord was still arriving — a slip — rather than merely whether it came up fast.

Found by a scripted scenario

Hands up is not chord over

Capture ended as soon as every key was released, even with the sustain pedal down. That finished the attempt on a note that was only a ringing remnant, before the real chord had been played.

With the pedal held, capture now waits for silence. It is also simply what the instrument does: the notes are still sounding.

What is not done

All sixteen phases are built. That is not the same as finished, and this is the difference.

  • Nothing has run on hardware. No tablet and no MIDI keyboard have ever been attached to this build environment. The manual device test plan is entirely outstanding and it is the release blocker.
  • The capture thresholds have never been played on. An 80 ms quiet window, a 300 ms roll, a 70 ms grace for a brushed key. All plausible, none measured against fingers — the specification itself calls them starting values for user testing.
  • No audio has been heard. The mixer is tested by rendering buffers and reading the samples back — envelope shape, voice stealing, the limiter, silence where silence belongs. That proves the arithmetic. It does not prove it sounds like a piano.
  • The release APK has never been installed. It builds, shrinks and packages at 3.9 MB. The ProGuard rules are therefore unproven against runtime reflection, and serialization and Room are exactly where that would show.
  • The database has never been closed and reopened. The encoding is tested and the exported schema runs against a real SQLite, but Room's own round trip needs an emulator.
  • Only chord gates are reachable from the app. Ear training, reading and progression gates have content, policies, engines and tests, and no screen launches them.
  • Export and import have no button. The service and its database layer exist and are tested; no settings screen calls either.
  • Half of the visual design does not exist. Two screens are approved as painted plates. The design system's tokens and all twenty-seven components are built and tested against them, and the remaining screens have not been designed, so there are no screenshot baselines to compare against.
  • Accessibility has been passed over one screen. The exercise screen. The campaign, progress, diagnostics and progression screens have not been audited.
  • The voice-leading gates use the wrong evaluator. They practise the right material as progressions; Region 13 asks for scoring on total motion, leaps and retained common tones, and the requirement type for that exists with no content field reaching it.
  • Region 0 is a chord exercise, not the note-on/off, sustain and latency check the curriculum describes.
  • Nobody has read the campaign. Every exercise is machine-checked as generable, spellable and playable. No person has read the forty-three previews or played a session.

Next

A tablet and a keyboard

  • Install it. Everything above can be built here; none of it can be judged here. The first session on real hardware will answer more questions than another phase would.
  • Tune what the hands say to tune. The capture thresholds, the 650 ms advance in Progression Run, and the sampler are three things that can only be set by somebody playing them.
  • Then the screens. The design system is ready for the design; the engines are ready for their screens; and the two are the same piece of work.
